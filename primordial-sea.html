An early ocean, seen through a porthole

Roughly sixty identical cells drift in a shallow primordial sea. Green drifts of nutrients grow and spread across the floor; a few hydrothermal vents glow at fixed points, feeding the water even in the dark.

Every cell carries a small genome — size, speed, senses, diet, aggression, a pull toward kin. When a cell has gathered enough energy it splits in two, passing its genome on with small random mutations. There is no script from here: whether its descendants stay solitary grazers, turn to hunting each other, cluster into colonies, or knit themselves into something like a simple body, is entirely up to what survives.

Click any cell to read its genome, edit it by hand, or clone it with a twist. Watch as long as you like — the sea keeps its own time.
